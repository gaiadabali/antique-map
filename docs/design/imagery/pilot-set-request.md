Dear {owner's name},

We are designing the new pages for both sites, and the photographs will decide how
good they look more than anything we draw. So before we design, we would like to work
with **a few real photographs of real pieces** — your pieces, photographed by you or
your staff.

This is also a test of our photography guide on your own equipment. Doing six items
first means any problem — the light, the colour, the files — is found now, not after
hundreds of photos.

## What we are asking for

**Six gallery items**, chosen by you, and **the Denpasar showroom**.

| # | Which item | Why |
| - | ---------- | --- |
| 1 | a map you are proud of | the gallery's page must do justice to your best |
| 2 | a second map, if possible **with hand colouring** | colour is the hardest thing to photograph truly |
| 3 | an engraved or etched print | its plate mark and lettering are what collectors check |
| 4 | a historic photograph, on its mount if it has one | photographs need their own care |
| 5 | any piece **with a visible defect** — a tear, a repair, foxing | so we design pages that show condition honestly |
| 6 | **one typical item exactly as it is on today's website** | most items will move to the new site with the photo they have now, so the design must work with it |

If one of your choices is **framed or in a mat**, even better — tell us whether it can
come out safely. Please **do not choose** one of the Bali Hotel posters: their rights
are still being checked, and nothing in the design may depend on them.

**For item 6, do not take a new photo.** Send the file you uploaded to the current
website, from your own computer or files — we do not take anything from the website
itself. If you no longer have it, tell us: it will come with the catalogue export
instead. Add its stock number and its text as you have it.

**For items 1–5**, the attached shot list for each kind of piece says which photos to
take. In short:

- the whole front and the whole back;
- two to four close-ups — and one of every defect you know of;
- one photo lit from the side (it shows the paper's surface);
- if the piece is framed and sold framed, one of the frame.

## The showroom

Photographed as a visitor meets it, on a bright day, with the lights you normally have
on. **In each room, take one photo of the colour card first**, held where that room's
photos will be; the room's other photos then need no card.

- the street and the entrance;
- a wide view from the door, and one from the back looking out;
- each wall, straight on, with its products and QR labels;
- the counter, and the place where online orders are collected;
- three to five close-ups — a shelf, a framed print on a wall, wrapped gifts.

**How the prints are made.** Thank you for agreeing that we may film and photograph
this. On the same day, please photograph it in the showroom, or wherever it happens:

- the original being photographed or scanned for printing;
- a print coming off the printer;
- a frame being joined, a print going into its mount and frame;
- a gift being wrapped, a parcel being packed.

Hands at work are welcome; a face only with that person's agreement, staff too. Handle
an original as carefully as on the photo table, and never in the sun. Here too, one
photo of the colour card first in each room. If you also film, keep the video files as
they are and tell us; we will say how to send them.

**If you have time — optional:**

- one wall with a framed print on it, from a tripod, straight on: first with two 1 m
  strips of tape on the wall — one level, one upright — then, without moving the
  tripod, the same photo without the tape. It may become a real room in the print
  configurator;
- two or three of your products — a framed print, a postcard set, a tote — as the
  product shot list describes.

## What you need

A phone from the last few years is enough. A camera, two daylight lamps or a colour
chart make it better, but please do not buy anything for this. What does matter:

- **daylight, never sun on an original**, and no ceiling lights mixed in;
- **a grey card or colour chart and a ruler beside the piece** in every photo of an
  item — except a photo lit from behind on a light pad, where the ruler alone is enough;
- in the showroom, and for products in a room or in hands, **one photo of the card at
  the start of each room or scene**, then the rest without it;
- the phone or camera held **straight above**, the whole piece in the picture;
- **no hands and no people** in the gallery photos.

## How to send them

- Please use **a shared folder** — Google Drive, Dropbox or whatever you use — shared
  with {our address}. Or reply and we will send you a folder link.
- **Please do not send them by WhatsApp or email.** Both shrink photos, and a shrunk
  photo cannot be used. If WhatsApp is the only way for a few, send them as a
  *Document*, not as a photo.
- One folder per item, named with its stock number, **the dot written as a hyphen**:
  M.9999 becomes `M-9999`. Item 6's folder starts with `legacy-`: `legacy-M-9997`. The
  showroom's photos go in a folder of their own, `showroom`; the handover guide shows
  the whole layout.
- In each item's folder, a few lines of notes: its size, any defects you know of,
  whether it is framed, and which phone or camera and light you used.
- **Please send item 1 first** and tell us. We will check it before you go on, so a
  problem is caught early.

## What we will do with them

- We copy them into **private storage**. Nothing is published.
- We check every photo and send you a short report: each one **passes**, is **fixed by
  us** (for example renamed), needs **one thing re-taken** — we say exactly what — or
  cannot be used, and why.
- We use them to design the new pages. You will see the designs on them, and so will a
  small group of test buyers; nothing goes on a public site until you approve it.
- We remove location data from anything that is ever published.

## Five quick questions

They help us set the guide to what you really have. Short answers are fine.

1. Who will take the photos, and with what — which phone or camera?
2. Do you have lamps, a tripod or stand, a grey card or a colour chart?
3. Can framed or matted pieces come out of their frames safely for photos?
4. Do any pieces already have high-resolution scans or professional photos?
5. Roughly how many pieces a day could your staff photograph, if this works?

If we could have the photographs by {date}, the design work can start on them straight
away. Thank you — this is the step that makes everything after it look right.
