# Photographing the shop — a guide for Old East Indies

*Bahasa Indonesia:* [shop-guide.id.md](shop-guide.id.md)

For the owner and staff of Old East Indies. The gallery photographs originals like
museum objects; the shop photographs **products in life** — in sunlight, in hands,
in rooms, wrapped as gifts, on the showroom walls. Warm, real and giftable, and
always honest about what the buyer will receive.

The shot list for a product is one page: [merch-product.md](shot-lists/merch-product.md).
The rules on retouching and on mockups are shared with the gallery:
[retouching-and-labelling.md](retouching-and-labelling.md).

## 1. The two kinds of product photo

| | Catalogue shots | Life shots |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What | the product alone: `flat`, `detail` | the product in use: `in-room`, `lifestyle`, `scale`, `packaging`, `showroom` |
| Why | the buyer sees exactly what arrives — colour, paper, frame, size | the buyer sees it in their life — on a wall, in a hand, as a gift |
| Light | even and neutral, like the gallery's | Bali daylight — sun, shade, rooms |
| Colour card | in every frame | one reference photo at the start of each scene |
| People | none | hands welcome; faces only with consent |

A product page needs at least a `flat`, an `in-room` and a `detail` image
(requirement 7.12). An `in-room` image may be a digital mockup until a real photo
replaces it — labelled as a mockup ([§8](#8-mockups-are-fine--labelled)).

## 2. What you need — good, better, best

| | Good | Better | Best |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Camera | a phone from the last few years, its main (1×) camera | a mirrorless or DSLR camera and a 50 mm or macro lens | the same, and a second lens for rooms (about 35 mm) |
| Catalogue light | a window with no sun through it, and a white reflector | two identical daylight lamps (5000–5600 K, CRI 95+) | the lamps with polarising filters, for glazed frames |
| Colour reference | an 18% grey card | a 24-patch colour chart | the same |
| Catalogue background | one plain, matte, light background — a sheet of card or cloth | a seamless paper roll | the same, always the same one |
| Support | a phone stand | a tripod | a tripod and a remote release |

The catalogue background's exact colour is chosen with the shop's look (phase 12).
Until then, use a plain off-white or light grey — the same one for every product.

## 3. Catalogue shots — the product as it arrives

- **`flat`** — the whole product, straight on, filling most of the frame, on the plain
  background, evenly lit, the colour card beside it. For a framed print: the whole
  frame, square on. For a postcard set: the set fanned or laid out. For a tote:
  laid flat or hung, with its handles.
- **`detail`** — close enough to see the paper texture, the print's sharpness, the
  frame's corner and joint, the mount's bevel, the stitching, the notebook's binding.
  This is where a buyer decides the quality is real.
- Lights, white balance and camera settings as in the gallery guide (§5–§6): one kind
  of light, the camera parallel to the product, the timer, the highest resolution.
- **The flat shot is the colour reference** for every other photo of that product. A
  life shot whose product looks a different colour from the flat shot is re-taken or
  corrected against it.

## 4. Life shots — sun, hands, rooms

**Light.** Bali daylight is the look. The best hours are the morning and late
afternoon; open shade (under a roof, beside a wall) gives true colours. Noon sun is
hard and makes glare on glazing — move into shade or turn the frame a little. The
warmth may come from the scene; **the product's own colours stay true** — check them
against the flat shot.

**The colour card, once per scene.** At the start of each new place or each change of
light, take one photo with the colour card held in the scene where the product will
be, then take the series. Name it as the scene's reference (see handover.md).

**Rooms (`in-room`).** A print on a real wall, a notebook on a real desk, coasters
on a real table: homes, villas, cafés, hotels, the showroom. Ask the place's owner
first. Keep other artworks, logos and brand names out of the frame.

**Hands and people (`lifestyle`).** A hand holding a postcard, a tote on a shoulder,
someone hanging a print. Hands need no release. **A recognisable face needs the
person's written consent**, and children only with a parent's. Staff too — ask them.

**Scale (`scale`).** Something whose size everyone knows, beside or holding the
product: a hand, a phone, an A4 sheet, a coffee cup, a sofa under a print. Never a
prop that makes the product look bigger than it is.

**Packaging (`packaging`).** The gift wrap — the cloth printed with an archive map —
wrapped and unwrapped; the gift note; the tube or box a print ships in; a parcel
ready to send. Buyers of gifts want to see what the recipient will open.

## 5. The showroom

The showroom is a stock location and a pickup point, and the shop's proof that it is
real. Photograph it as a visitor meets it; the pilot request lists the shots:

- the street and the entrance, so a visitor recognises it on arrival;
- a wide view from the door, and one from the back looking out;
- each wall, straight on, with its products and QR labels;
- the counter, and the place where online orders are collected;
- a few close vignettes: a shelf, a framed print on a wall, a stack of wrapped gifts.

Shoot on a bright day with the lights the showroom normally has on. **In each room,
take one reference photo first** — the colour card held where that room's photos will
be — then the room's photos without it. Name it `showroom_ref_<nn>`: it is kept, never
published (intake-spec.md §1, §6).

**How the prints are made (`making`).** The owner agreed to it (interview question
S14): on the photo day, photograph how a print is made from an original, in the
showroom or wherever it happens —

- the original being photographed or scanned for printing;
- a print coming off the printer;
- a frame being joined, a print going into its mount and frame;
- a gift being wrapped in the map cloth, a parcel being packed.

Hands at work are welcome; a recognisable face needs consent, staff included (§4).
An original is handled as on the gallery's table — clean, dry hands, nothing on the
printed area, never in the sun ([gallery-guide.md](gallery-guide.md) §4–§5). The same
reference photo starts each room. Name the photos `showroom_making_<nn>`
([handover.md](handover.md) §2); one that shows a single product may be
`<product>_lifestyle_<nn>` instead ([merch-product.md](shot-lists/merch-product.md)).
If you also film, keep the video files as they are and tell us — this guide covers
photographs; we will say how to send video.

## 6. Respect and the brand's lines

- Celebrate the archipelago and its cartography — **not the VOC**. No VOC logo, no
  colonial costume, pith helmets or "old colonial" styling as props.
- Offerings, shrines, temples and ceremonies are not props: never place products on
  or against them.
- People are shown as themselves, never as decoration or costume.
- The Hofker Bali Hotel posters are not used as the lead image of anything until
  their rights are confirmed (D6).

## 7. What a shop photo may never do

- Change the product: its colour, paper, frame finish, size or material.
- Show an option that does not exist, or a product not yet made, as if it were real
  (that is a mockup — §8).
- Hide a fault in the sample. If the sample has a flaw the real product will not have,
  photograph a good one.

Cleaning a photo is fine: dust, lint, a stray thread, a reflection of the
photographer in the glass, a tidier background. Details in
[retouching-and-labelling.md](retouching-and-labelling.md).

**Restoring the artwork is a different job.** Removing foxing, tears and fading from
the print file a reproduction is made from is allowed, within limits, and every
product says what was done (retouching-and-labelling.md §2). It is never done to a
photograph of the original — the gallery's photographs show the original as it is.

## 8. Mockups are fine — labelled

A mockup is a picture made on a computer: a print placed digitally into a room, a
tote drawn on a model. It is quick, and it lets a product launch before it has been
photographed. **Every mockup is labelled as a digital mockup** on the site, and a
real photograph replaces it when one exists. The configurator's rooms are mockups
too, and say so ([room-scenes.md](room-scenes.md)).

## 9. Sending

Keep the original files; name them and send them through the shared folder, as
[handover.md](handover.md) explains — **never through WhatsApp**, which shrinks
every photo. Location data is removed before anything is published.
